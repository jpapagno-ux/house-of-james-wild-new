A SNORKELLING & 
DIVING PARADISE
In remote Southeast Sulawesi lies the 
spectacular Wakatobi National Marine Park, 
its name a portmanteau of the four main 
Tukangbesi Islands: Wangi-Wangi, 
Kaledupa, Tomia and Binongko. 
Wakatobi comprises 1.4 million hectares, 
900,000 hectares of which are covered 
with vibrant, multi-coloured coral reefs that 
are abundant in fish, sharks, turtles, dolphins 
and whales – making this one of the best 
sites for snorkelling and diving anywhere 
in the world.
There are treasures to be found above 
the waters too – with floating villages  
and tribes of Bajo people (seafaring 
nomads, famed for their free-diving fishing 
skills), making this an off-the-beaten-path 
destination perfect for underwater 
adventurers and cultural travellers alike.

===== PAGE 3 =====
AN UNDERWATER 
NIRVANA…
…is how Wakatobi was described by the 
famous French oceanographer Jacques 
Cousteau who marvelled at its 25 groups 
of coral reefs – including atolls, barrier 
reefs and fringing reefs – which are 
home to 750 of the world’s 850 corals. 
In comparison, the Red Sea has just 300 
species, and the Caribbean a mere 50. 
The multi-coloured marine wonderland 
includes coral types as varied as torch, 
table, leather, cactus, elkhorn, smooth-
cauliflower, sun, wrinkle and brain, and 
is home to vast seagrass meadows and 
more than 900 species of fish.

DAY 2
Where we go
Liwutonkidi Island – also known as Snake Island, although  
rest assured, it’s not an island full of snakes, just a wealth of 
natural beauty.
What you’ll do
Spend as much time as you like on the picture-perfect 
white-sand beach. After a gourmet picnic lunch, slowly drift 
back to the boat by kayak.
What you’ll see
Baby blacktip sharks and blue-spotted stingrays on the  
island’s pristine reefs.
The highlight
Your very own private, pop-up beach club complete with  
sun loungers, parasols, ice-cold face towels and fresh  
coconut water. 
Try your hand at
A wealth of watersports; if you’re feeling energetic, try and  
break the Rascal record for holding on to the flying mattress  
or take the 7m-high jump off the boat’s roof deck.

DAY 3 
Where we go
Wangi-Wangi Island, the gateway to the Wakatobi Islands and 
home to the Wangi-Wangi white-eye, an endemic songbird 
species that sports plumage resembling white spectacles.
What you’ll do
Spend the day diving and snorkelling along the island’s western 
reefs; visit Mola Village and watch how the sea people free-dive 
using their own handmade goggles.
What you’ll see
The huge array of fish hiding amongst the pillars of Sombu 
Jetty, as well as the pelagic species out on the reef including 
barracudas and eagle rays.
The highlight
Interacting with the people of the village and shopping for 
Wakatobi handicrafts; diving the incredible diversity of marine 
landscapes that include underwater plains and hidden caves.
Try your hand at
In between snorkels and dives, join one of our private chefs on 
board for an Indonesian cooking class and learn how to make 
our world-famous spicy sambal.

DAY 4
where we go
Moromaho Island, a tiny island just 3km wide, famous for its 
thousands of coconut palms.
What you’ll do
Go on a gentle, early morning kayak among the coastal 
mangroves; snorkel or dive and spot large schools of fish –  
such as jacks and fusiliers – amongst the barrel sponges, 
gorgonians and black corals.
What you’ll see
A wonderfully diverse array of birds including red footed 
boobies, parakeets and great frigate birds.
The highlight
Enjoy a stroll on the beach then watch the sun set over the  
Banda Sea from the sand; or set up an easel on the roof deck 
and capture the same view in watercolours.
Try your hand at
Read up on the extraordinary legacy of French oceanographer 
Jacques Cousteau, who had his basecamp on the island,  
from where he explored the region’s rich diving grounds back  
in the 1980s.

DAY 5
Where we go
Tomia Island, the most famous diving destination in Wakatobi.
What you’ll do
Swim, dive and snorkel on Onemobaa Reef, named as one of 
the ‘best shore dives in the world’; walk up to the viewpoint at 
Kahyangan Peak for panoramic views of the surrounding islands.
What you’ll see
Pristine, hard coral reef plateaus, with schooling damselfish, 
cuttlefish, sweetlips, dottybacks and colourful feather stars.
The highlight
Taking a night dive or snorkel into ‘The Zoo’ and illuminating 
incredible nocturnal marine life with your torchlight.
Try your hand at
If you’ve ever been interested in trying underwater photography, 
this is the place to get started!

EXTEND YOUR VOYAGE
We recommend a minimum of 7 nights to discover 
Wakatobi, but this UNESCO-listed marine paradise 
can easily be explored for longer by those seeking 
more underwater adventures, or more time to relax  
in between excursions. 
+1 NIGHT
Explore more of the reefs around Tomia such as 
Teluk Maya – rich in staghorn, gorgonian and 
sea whip corals, and home to yellow trumpetfish, 
blue spotted rays and orangutan crabs. Dive at 
Palahidu Beach on Binongko – for a great chance of 
spotting sea turtles.
+2 nights
Enjoy more beach, dive and snorkel time in the northeast 
of the marine national park around the tranquil tropical 
islands of Anano and Runduma, both havens for a wealth 
of wildlife, especially sea turtles.

GETTING THERE
The voyage commences in Bau-Bau and ends in Kendari 
(or vice versa). There are multiple flights to both points of 
embarkation and disembarkation from Bali and Jakarta – 
with just a short connection required in Makassar.
The port of Bau-Bau is a 20-minute drive from the airport 
(BUW); Kendari port is a 40-minute drive from the airport 
(KDI). Our crew will collect you from the airport or your 
local hotel in Sulawesi.
The voyage begins at 3pm on the first day and ends at 1pm 
on the final day, so flights should be scheduled accordingly.
If you require assistance with flight schedules, flight 
bookings (including both commercial and private), 
and itinerary extensions, please don’t hesitate to ask our 
friendly team who will be more than happy to make your 
travel plans as seamless as possible.
