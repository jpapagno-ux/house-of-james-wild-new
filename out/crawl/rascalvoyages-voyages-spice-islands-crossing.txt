 EXPLORE THE SPICE ISLANDS 
 SPICE ISLANDS, RASCAL-STYLE
 Snorkel and dive the undersea drop-offs covered in multi-coloured coral gardens, home to huge, 3m-wide barrel sponges, some more than 1,500 years old; head to Banda Neira's vibrant fish market and shop for tuna and mackerel with our head chef who'll then prepare you a special, ocean-fresh lunch; for the fit and adventurous, climb the 656m-high, still-active Gunung Api Volcano—which last erupted in 1988 and whose lava is still clearly visible; pay a visit to the traditional village of Ameth—with special permission granted from the local king.
 WILDLIFE HIGHLIGHT: HAMMERHEAD SHARKS
 One of the newer-evolved shark species at around 20–25 million years old, the hammerhead is so named for its distinctively shaped head, known as a cephalofoil, which gives superior all-round vision as well as aiding navigation and maximising the shark's ability to find prey. They'll even use their heads to ram and pin down their favourite meal: stingrays. Hammerheads aren't aggressive towards humans, and we see shark congregations in their hundreds in the Banda Sea every year between September and November.  
 SIGNATURE EXPERIENCE: SPICEWORLD
 Take an immersive trip back in time as we learn about the local nutmeg—once hailed as a wonder drug with aphrodisiacal and hallucinogenic powers. You'll be able to pick your own spices at a nutmeg plantation, where we'll enjoy a breakfast complete with mace jam, spiced pastries, and iced cinnamon tea. Finally, we end the day with a pop-up dinner under the stars on top of Fort Belgica—built by the Dutch in 1611 in their efforts to control the so-called Spice Race—which boasts spectacular views of the surrounding islands and volcanoes.

 EASTERN LESSER SUNDA
 This spectacular voyage from Flores Island to the less-explored Alor archipelago encapsulates the best of Indonesia—featuring some of the country's most magnificent volcanic scenery, fascinating glimpses into tribal life, and myriad underwater treasures including reef sharks, bottlenose, spinner, and Fraser dolphins, vast populations of clownfish, and one of the highest and most colourful concentrations of anemones in the world.
