TIME TRAVEL 
IN STYLE
Between the 16th and 18th centuries, 
the Spice Islands were the centre of 
the known world – as the only source of 
mace, cinnamon, cloves and nutmeg, 
the latter a commodity considered more 
valuable than gold. 
There is a palpable sense of history to be 
found here that dates back to the era of 
the Spice Wars when European empires 
fought for control of the lucrative spice 
trade, and when the Dutch famously 
swapped Manhattan with the British in 
return for nutmeg-covered Run Island.

DAY 1
Where we go
Ambon to Nusa Laut, a small inhabited island east of Ambon.
What you’ll do
Snorkel or dive the majestic reef of Nusa Laut which is protected 
by the locals; visit the sustainable fishing village of Ameth – with 
permission from the local king.
What you’ll see
A jaw-dropping array of hard corals including fan corals, 
metre-wide sponge corals and even larger plate corals that are 
home to a diverse range of colourful reef fish, including angelfish 
and butterflyfish.
The highlight
Head to the roof deck to enjoy your first Rascal sunset (and 
sundowner) in the middle of the Banda Sea; from here, you’ll be 
able to spot pods of dolphins – sometimes in their hundreds.
Try your hand at
After a gourmet lunch, learn how to make Rascal’s world-famous 
sambal (an addictively spicy condiment) from one of our two 
private onboard chefs.

DAY 2
Where we go
Banda Neira, the main island of the Banda archipelago.
What you’ll do
Explore the main town with its lively fish market, spice 
plantations and fascinating historical monuments; snorkel and 
dive a beautiful offshore hard coral garden nourished by the 
surrounding volcanic rock.
What you’ll see
On land: locals using gai pala (nutmeg pickers) to pull down the 
fruit; the imposing hilltop Fort Belgica, built by the Dutch in the 
17th century. Underwater: the wealth of marine life including 
endemic black butterflyfish, crocodilefish, spotted eagle rays and 
broadclub cuttlefish.
The highlight
Enjoying an exclusive pop-up dinner under the stars atop 
Fort Belgica.
Try your hand at
Tasting mace jam, cinnamon tea, and our favourite freshly grated 
nutmeg on a Painkiller cocktail – a delicious blend of rum, cream 
of coconut, pineapple and orange juice served on the rocks.

DAY 3
Where we go
The island of Ai which boasts just one village surrounded by 
nutmeg and almond plantations, and the historic Run Island.
What you’ll do
Ai and Run are both superb sites for snorkelling and diving, with 
a mix of shallow reefs, sheer coral walls and deep waters that are 
abundant with marine life.
What you’ll see
Eagle rays, reef sharks, dogtooth tuna, trevallies, wahoo, rays, 
bumphead parrotfish and black snappers, amongst many others.
The highlight
If you’re lucky, being checked out by curious – but friendly – 
hammerhead sharks as you join them in the deep blue.
Try your hand at
A sunset snorkel or dive at the Banda Neira Jetty to try and spot 
the elaborate courtship ritual of the mandarin fish – when pairs 
rise up through the water together cheek-to-cheek as if slow 
dancing.

DAY 4
where we go
The snorkelling and diving hotspots of Hatta Island and 
Batu Kapal (Batu means ‘stone’ and Kapal means ‘ship’ – 
the perfect description of this tiny island shaped like a ship).
What you’ll do
Explore the islands’ various underwater landscapes: the coral 
formations at Batu Kapal are spectacular, with a stunning variety 
of hard and soft corals; while Hatta is famous for its vibrant and 
very steep coral reef drop-offs.
What you’ll see
Spotted soapfish, barracudas, grey reef sharks, angelfish, 
pyramid butterflyfish, surgeonfish and green chromis.
The highlight
Watching the fascinating moray eels emerge from their hiding 
spots at the same time. At first they might appear dangerous 
because they constantly open their large mouths, but this is not 
a hostile gesture, simply the way they breathe. 
Try your hand at
Practice your free diving skills by holding your breath for up to 
3 minutes, then dive in.

DAY 5
Where we go
Suanggi Island. Its name might translate as Evil Spirit Island, 
but it’s in fact a stand-out snorkelling and dive site, with a 
spectacularly beautiful coral reef.
What you’ll do
Snorkel or dive the waters off Suanggi Island, well-known for the 
diverse underwater topography including vibrant shallows, steep 
sloping reefs, ridges, pinnacles and walls.
What you’ll see
An incredibly rich marine micro-ecosystem, home to schools 
of fusiliers, whitetip reef sharks, moray eels, day octopus, and if 
you’re lucky, solitary or schooling hammerhead sharks.
The highlight
The remoteness of an island where few travellers get to 
venture. Apart from Suanggi’s marine treasures, there is superb 
birdwatching here too.
Try your hand at
A brand-new watersport – whether it’s stand-up paddleboarding, 
kayaking or trying to break the Rascal record of holding on to the 
flying mattress for as long as possible.

DAY 6
where we go
Pombo Island, an uninhabited paradise island surrounded by 
a rocky coral atoll.
What you’ll do
Enjoy a leisurely day on the island, part of the Pombo Island 
Marine Recreation Park, which encompasses 1,000 hectares of 
white-sand beaches, virgin jungle and crystal-clear waters.
What you’ll see
A wealth of marine life including moray eels, spotted eagle rays, 
hawksbill turtles and reef octopus.
The highlight
Your very own private, pop-up beach club complete with sun 
loungers, parasols, ice-cold face towels and fresh coconut water.
Try your hand at
Take a guided jungle safari to try and spot the exotic birds that 
perch on the tree branches.

EXTEND YOUR VOYAGE
We recommend a minimum of 7 nights to discover the 
Spice Islands, but the region can easily be explored for 
longer by those seeking more underwater and cultural 
adventures, or if you’re looking for more time to relax in 
between excursions.
+1 NIGHT
Sail to the most remote island of the Banda Sea: 
the uninhabited, volcanic Manuk Island. Once the source 
of sulphur extracted by Chinese traders, it is now better 
known for its hundreds of friendly Chinese sea snakes, 
vibrant corals and hammerhead sharks.
+3 NIGHTS
Cruise to Misool, home to the finest soft coral gardens and 
most abundant reef life in the world; see the spectacular 
3,000 to 5,000-year-old ochre petroglyphs (rock paintings) 
which are amazingly well preserved. This extension finishes 
in Sorong (West Papua).
+5 NIGHTS
The ultimate Spice Islands crossing culminating with the 
highlights of Raja Ampat: Misool and the Dampier Strait – 
a world-renowned dive site, which also boasts beautiful 
mangroves. This extension finishes in Sorong (West Papua).

GETTING THERE
This special Spice Islands voyage 
begins and ends in Ambon. 
There are multiple weekly flights 
from Jakarta to Ambon’s Pattimura 
International Airport, which is a short 
25-minute drive away from Telehu, 
the port of embarkation. Our crew will 
collect you from the airport or your 
accommodation in Ambon. 
The voyage begins at 8am on the 
first day and ends at 11am on the 
final day, so flights should be scheduled 
accordingly.
If you require assistance with flight 
schedules, flight bookings (including 
both commercial and private), and 
itinerary extensions, please don’t hesitate 
to ask our friendly team who will be more 
than happy to make your travel plans as 
seamless as possible.
