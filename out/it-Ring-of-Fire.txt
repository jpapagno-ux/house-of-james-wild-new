ESCAPE TO THE 
RING OF FIRE
Located in the southwest corner of  
the Pacific Ring of Fire, Indonesia is  
one of the most volcanically active 
regions on Earth. Of the country’s  
more than 130 active or dormant 
volcanoes, the vast majority are  
located within the so-called Sunda  
Arc; and this voyage encompasses  
an extraordinary stretch of that arc 
between the islands of Sumbawa 
and Flores – a region of spectacular 
landscapes that teem with wildlife, 
both above and below the waves.

DAY 1 
Where we go
Moyo Island, part of the Moyo Satonda National Park; its entire 
coastline has been declared a marine conservation area.
What you’ll do
Offshore: snorkel and dive on Angel Reef, well-known for its 
steep coral wall which descends over 40m and is covered in 
colourful staghorn, brain and sheet corals. Onshore: experience 
village life on Moyo Island, home to the best honey in Indonesia.
What you’ll see
Alongside red-toothed triggerfish, angelfish, butterflyfish, 
clownfish and batfish, there are Kuhl’s stingrays hiding in the 
sand, while the coral’s nooks and crannies are home to moray 
eels, frogfish, and pygmy seahorses.
The highlight
Jump on the back of a local’s motorbike and visit Mata Jitu,  
a beautiful waterfall with cascading pools, also called the  
Lady Diana Falls because of her visit here in 1993.
Try your hand at
Becoming a true Rascal by jumping off the 7m roof deck.

===== PAGE 5 =====
DAY 2
Where we go
Teluk Saleh, and the volcanic island of Satonda.
What you’ll do
Snorkel with whale sharks in Teluk Saleh; trek to the top of the 
spectacular dormant volcano of Satonda, which rises from a 
depth of 1,000m underwater.
What you’ll see
Snorkellers will get to spot colourful nudibranchs, pygmy 
pipehorses and lionfish; while trekkers will see long-tailed 
macaques and thousands of nocturnal fruit bats (flying foxes). 
The highlight
Kayaking on the waters of the lagoon inside Satonda’s crater. 
According to popular stories, the lagoon once held fresh water, 
but the giant eruption of nearby Mount Tambora in 1815 created 
a tidal wave so immense that it filled the crater with sea water. 
Try your hand at
With some simple underwater photography, support marine 
biologists in their quest to study population numbers and 
discover new migration routes of whale sharks.

===== PAGE 7 =====
DAY 4
where we go
Batu Monco Bay, and the islands of Gili Lawa Laut and Darat.
What you’ll do
Snorkel or dive some of the most idyllic coral reefs in Indonesia 
including the fabled Ten Foot Reef and Shotgun; trek to the 
top of Gili Lawa Darat for some of the best and most unspoiled 
sunset views of the national park.
What you’ll see
Hawksbill turtles, manta rays, glassfish, golden sweepers,  
lionfish, damselfish, pygmy seahorses and cuttlefish.
The highlight
Experiencing the explosive current of the so-called ‘Shotgun’, 
where you are propelled through a channel between two  
islands at the speed of a manta ray.
Try your hand at
A cooking class with one of Rascal’s private chefs on  
board where you can learn how to make our world-famous  
spicy sambal.

DAY 5
Where we go
Loh Liang in Komodo Island, and Padar.
What you’ll do
Take an early morning walking safari in search of the famous 
Komodo dragons; visit Pink Beach and snorkel or dive its waters; 
take a sunset trek to the top of Padar Besar with its panoramic 
views of the national park.
What you’ll see
A wealth of wildlife such as Komodo dragons, water buffaloes, 
Timor deer, wild boars and yellow-crested cockatoos. 
The highlight
If you’re lucky, at least a handful of Komodo dragons, the largest 
living lizard in the world.
Try your hand at
Learn how to navigate the old way with maps, and head to the 
bridge to take supervised control of the ship’s wheel.

DAY 6
where we go
The islands of Taka Makassar, Siaba Besar and Seraya.
What you’ll do
Dive or snorkel the stunning reefs including Taka Makassar  
(the longest coral reef in Komodo National Park), and Siaba 
Besar, also known as Turtle City, due to the abundance of turtles.
What you’ll see
Hawksbill turtles, black and whitetip reef sharks, trevallies, 
blue-spotted stingrays, oriental sweetlips, bigeye soldierfishes, 
flounders, pufferfish and flying gurnards.
The highlight
Your very own pop-up restaurant on a secluded beach where 
you’ll experience our signature dinner under the stars.
Try your hand at
After dinner, learn some Indonesian songs and dance moves  
to the rhythms of the Rascal in-house band.

EXTEND YOUR VOYAGE
We recommend a minimum of 6 nights to discover the Ring of 
Fire, but it can be explored for up to 12 nights for those seeking 
a longer adventure or more time to relax in between activities. 
+1 night
Spend time underwater exploring more of the signature dive 
and snorkel sites of Komodo National Park.
+2 nights
Spend a day at Mount Sangeang, an active volcano. Watch the 
local fishermen building traditional phinisi boats on the 
black-sand beach; dive or snorkel the ‘Hot Rocks’, where volcanic 
gasses rise from the seabed and the corals flourish in the 
nutrient-rich waters.
+3 nightS
Visit both Mount Sangeang and the island of Nusa Kode –  
where you can see Komodo dragons swimming from the beach 
towards you. (Don’t worry – you’ll be safe in the tender!)
+6 nights 
Follow the route of volcanoes back to Sumbawa including the 
best of Komodo, remote treasures like Kelapa Island, Gili Banta, 
and Saringi with thrilling fishing experiences, secluded beaches, 
and captivating villages waiting to be explored.

GETTING THERE
This Ring of Fire voyage begins in Sumbawa Besar  
and ends in Komodo National Park, but can be taken  
in either direction (please enquire for further details).
The port of Sumbawa is just a short 15-minute drive  
from Sumbawa Besar airport which is served by  
flights from Denpasar, Bali and the island of Lombok. 
Our crew will collect you from the airport or your local 
accommodation. The airport for Komodo National Park 
is Komodo International Airport located in the town of 
Labuan Bajo, which is served by regular flights from  
Jakarta and Denpasar.
The voyage begins in Sumbawa Besar at 11am on  
the first day and ends at 1pm on the final day so flights 
should be scheduled accordingly.
If you require assistance with flight schedules, flight 
bookings (including both commercial and private),  
and itinerary extensions, please don’t hesitate to ask  
our friendly team who will be more than happy to make 
your travel plans as seamless as possible.
